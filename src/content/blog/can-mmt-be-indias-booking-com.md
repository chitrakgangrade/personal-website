*Originally written Oct 2, 2024. Addendum added Aug 2026.*

## Summary

MakeMyTrip (MMT) is well-positioned to capitalize on India's rapidly growing travel sector. As the dominant player in the market[^1], MMT has built strong foundations through strategic investments in technology, pricing engines, branding, marketing and loyalty programs. We believe Indian domestic touring is going to skyrocket -- due to increasing incomes, the inelasticity of travel[^2], and improving Indian infrastructure which will convert into a flywheel. With the increasing demand for domestic tourism and improving infrastructure in India, MMT is primed to benefit from this surge, offering investors long-term growth potential. Despite some concerns about overvaluation, MMT's unique position, market leadership, and diverse offerings make it a compelling buy, especially on price dips.

## Why We Think MMT Is a Strong Buy

- **Consolidated market.** MMT has emerged as a leader in India's highly fragmented travel industry, benefiting from years of investment in foundational technology like pricing engines, brand, loyalty programs, and unique deals. This first-mover advantage is crucial now that the market has consolidated, positioning MMT to take full advantage of favorable macroeconomic conditions and growing travel demand.
- **Multiple vertical offerings.** Unlike global players such as Booking.com, which derive most of their revenue from hotels, MMT has diversified across multiple verticals like airlines, buses, cabs and holiday packages. While this diversification was initially due to the lack of depth in the Indian hotel industry, it now provides MMT with a unique advantage, allowing it to engage customers across the travel spectrum and drive higher customer retention. As India's hotel industry expands, MMT will experience double compounding -- growing revenue from both hotels and its auxiliary offerings.
- **Well-positioned for tailwinds.** MMT is now in a phase where it stands to benefit from India's growing infrastructure investments and the rise in domestic tourism. As previously inaccessible destinations become weekend trips, MMT's established relationships with local suppliers and travel ecosystems will allow it to capture the market as more travelers come online.
- **Strategic US listing.** MMT's listing on the U.S. markets might work to its advantage. While it doesn't receive as much attention as it would on Indian exchanges, this listing could provide hidden value for long-term investors. As India's travel market booms and global investors seek exposure, MMT might attract significant investment as a proxy for the country's tourism growth.
- **Improving margins and growth trajectory.** MMT's financials show improving EBITDA margins, driven by operational efficiency and market consolidation. As the company continues to grow, it is expected to improve profitability further, replicating the success of global travel giants like Booking.com.

## Key Assumptions

- **Travel demand is inelastic.** As disposable incomes rise, travel becomes an essential expenditure, reinforced by social factors like the influence of social media. People may forgo other discretionary spending but still prioritize travel.
- **High margins post-market consolidation.** MMT is expected to negotiate better deals with suppliers (hotels, airlines, etc.), driving profitability as the market consolidates. This mirrors the experience of other dominant online travel platforms globally -- we've seen BKNG provide similar margins.
- **India's tourism potential.** The long-term potential of India's tourism market remains a key assumption. With improving infrastructure and accessibility, more people will travel, both domestically and internationally, driving MMT's growth.

## Key Risks with This Strategy

- **Over-reliance on vertical integration.** One key concern is that MMT had to invest in multiple categories (airlines, buses) because the hotel industry in India wasn't large enough. This differs from global peers like Booking.com, which primarily focused on hotels. While MMT's auxiliary offerings may engage users, there's a risk that the hotel industry may not grow fast enough to drive meaningful profits like its global counterparts. The average order value for hotels in India is a fraction of what it is in Europe -- however, we believe the hotel sector will expand, allowing MMT to experience growth from multiple verticals.
- **Potential overvaluation.** MMT's P/E ratio of 54 indicates that it may be overvalued, especially as the stock has risen sharply in recent months. However, the company has just turned profitable, and as earnings continue to improve, the P/E ratio will naturally decline. Investors should consider buying on dips as MMT transitions into a more mature phase with consistent profitability.
- **Competitive pressure.** While the market has consolidated, future risks from well-funded competitors (e.g., Jio or Softbank-backed ventures) could disrupt MMT's market share. These players might reintroduce aggressive discounting strategies or bring new technology-driven innovations to the market.
- **Price sensitivity.** India is a price-sensitive market. This could potentially squeeze the suppliers and eventually MMT margins due to pressure from end customers. However, if you are a monopoly, you control the market and can control margins especially during high-demand holiday seasons -- and India is also a supply-constrained market when it comes to quality accommodation.

## Financial Balance Sheet Overview

- **Revenue growth.** MMT's revenue for the trailing twelve months stands at $840.31 million, up 29.87% year-over-year ([Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mmyt/revenue/)). This showcases strong demand across all its business segments, driven by post-pandemic recovery and increased domestic travel.
- **EBITDA margin.** The company's improving EBITDA margins reflect a path toward sustainable profitability. With adjusted EBIT at $124.2 million, MMT is moving toward long-term profitability, mirroring the trajectory of its global peers.
- **P/E ratio.** With a P/E ratio of 54, MMT appears expensive, but this is typical for companies that have recently turned profitable. As the company scales and earnings grow, the P/E ratio is expected to decline over time, providing better value for long-term investors.
- **Market cap.** As of October 2024, MMT's market cap is $10.06 billion, indicating strong investor confidence in its growth prospects ([Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mmyt/revenue/)). However, Booking is at ~$130bn with 15-20x the revenue. Can MMT reach there to get a multi-bagger return? It would boil down to the Indian economy being the same size as the European economy. Is that possible?

In conclusion, MakeMyTrip's unique position in the rapidly expanding Indian travel market, combined with its diversified offerings and strategic investments, make it a strong buy for investors looking for long-term growth. While some risks, like overvaluation and market competition, exist, MMT's potential for significant upside, driven by India's booming travel infrastructure and increasing tourism demand, outweighs these concerns.

## Notes and Updates

A running log of counterpoints and check-ins since the original thesis, kept rather than cleaned up -- the messiness is part of the record.

**Counter from Vibhor:** all supply-focused businesses will become an API, with an AI agent sitting on top of it controlling demand. (See [Nikesh Arora at All-In Summit 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q7sSi9h6nYM&t=1463s).)

**Counter from Manas:** too big a bet just on the India growth story.

**Notes from the Q2 FY2025 earnings call**

- Revenue increased 25.1% year-over-year to $211.0 million; gross bookings grew 22.7% to $2.26 billion; adjusted operating profit up 32.9% to $37.5 million; adjusted net profit up 46.3% to $40.7 million.
- Segment performance: air ticketing revenue up 25.6% to $61.0 million, hotels and packages up 15.5% to $103.2 million, bus ticketing up 25.4% to $24.8 million, other revenue up 100.8% to $22.0 million.
- Key metrics: air ticketing segments up 15.6% to 15.1 million, hotel room nights up 17.5% to 8.5 million, bus tickets traveled up 21.4% to 23.6 million.
- Margins held stable across segments -- air ticketing at 6.8%, hotels and packages at 17.5%.
- Cash and cash equivalents of $717.2 million as of September 30, 2024.
- Management remained positive on long-term growth prospects for India's travel market, focused on expanding supply partnerships and deepening customer engagement.

A few things that stood out from that call:

1. MMT's diversified business model -- loyalty programs, airlines, and more -- is something Booking Holdings is only starting to develop, and it's expected to help with compounding growth.
2. International business has emerged as a crucial, rapidly growing segment, potentially positioning MMT as India's equivalent of Ctrip: international air ticketing and outbound hotel revenue grew 39% and 62% year-on-year respectively, now 38% of air-ticketing and 17% of hotels adjusted margins, with over 32,000 international hotels across 2,384 cities in 165 countries.
3. Strong financial position with over $700 million in cash, plus an ongoing share repurchase program (up to $136 million in shares/convertible notes, $60 million annual limit on shares) -- conservative so far, but management remains committed.
4. Domestic air market share exceeding 30%, outpacing overall market growth despite supply challenges.
5. Potential supply issues in the domestic air market: limited fleet capacity, route constraints, airline-specific problems (like Go First's suspension), seasonal factors, and regulatory effects on flight supply.

**9 Dec 2025**

- The stock price has fallen over the last few months. The earnings calls haven't been as lucrative -- the growth expected isn't coming, and MMT has only grown in single digits over the last two quarters.
- International business is still growing. Indians traveling abroad -- especially to Southeast Asia -- is a big market, and MMT is taking a good share of it.
- Airlines have been messing up numbers: supply issues, the IndiGo fiasco, a terrorist attack, and war with Pakistan causing short-term volatility.
- Can the growth ever recover? The INR is also losing power -- maybe it's harder to travel abroad now. How many more years before growth comes back? Or is the India growth story a pipe dream?

## Addendum -- Thesis Review, Aug 2026

*Reviewed against Q4 FY26 (19 May 2026) and Q1 FY27 (3 Aug 2026) results. Price at review: $60.91, market cap ~$5.7bn, vs ~$10.1bn when this thesis was written.*

### What held

- **Diversification (see above).** The strongest pillar, and vindicated. Q1 FY27 adjusted margin growth in constant currency: Bus +32.4%, Others +27.2%, Hotels & Packages +21.3%, Air +10.8%. Ground transport and domestic absorbed the shock from international air. Note the reversal: this was listed as *Risk #1* ("over-reliance on vertical integration") and became the hedge.
- **Operating leverage.** FY26 EBIT $156m, +30.1% YoY on ~11% cc revenue growth. Adjusted operating margin expanded to 1.82% of gross bookings from 1.71%. FY26 gross bookings hit a record $10.4bn.
- **Overvaluation risk.** Correctly flagged at P/E 54, and it resolved -- brutally. The de-rating from ~$12.6bn (May 2025) to ~$3.9bn (Mar 2026) happened *while bookings grew to a record*. Multiple compression, not business deterioration.

### What broke

- **"P/E will naturally decline as earnings improve."** Mechanism suspended. The ~$3.1bn raised in 2025 to buy back Trip.com's stake left convertible notes due 2030 whose interest now consumes net income: Q1 FY27 net finance costs $28.3m, IFRS profit $9.1m vs $25.8m, EPS $0.09 vs $0.22. Trailing P/E of 175-260x is not a usable signal.
- **"Strategic US listing is hidden value."** Management disagrees. MMT India pre-filed a confidential DRHP with SEBI on 17 July 2026 (BSE/NSE, up to ~$1bn, primarily an OFS -- no fresh capital to the company). The visibility discount was real enough to fix. Creates a possible holdco discount at the parent.
- **Competitive framing.** This thesis named Jio and SoftBank as the threat. The actual pressure is Booking.com/Agoda, plus Trip.com -- which cut its stake from ~45% to 16.9% and is now hiring in India to compete directly.

### What was missing entirely

- **Currency.** Q1 FY27: gross bookings +19.9% cc became +6.2% reported revenue growth on \>10% INR depreciation. For a USD-listed India proxy, FX is a permanent structural drag on reported results, independent of operations.
- **Regulatory / margin quality.** Morpheus Research (30 Mar 2026, 103 interviews, disclosed short) alleges price parity clauses persist despite the 2022 CCI order, a $212m gap between adjusted and IFRS profit since 2021, an undisclosed CCI "hub-and-spoke" investigation, and a $20m Go Air receivable peers have written off. The ₹223.48 crore fine remains under NCLAT appeal with only 10% deposited. MMT declined to respond substantively. *This is the most important revision to the thesis.* The original risk section argued: "if you are a monopoly, you control the market and can control margins." That is precisely the conduct the CCI ruled illegal and Morpheus alleges continues. If pricing power is partly regulatory-contingent rather than earned, the BKNG margin analogy is weaker than assumed.
- **AI disintermediation.** Vibhor's counter above has moved from footnote to central question. MMT's answer is Myra 2.0 -- 85,000 daily conversations, 45%+ from Tier II/III cities, resolving \>50% of post-booking queries, agentic payments in eight languages. Whether an OTA can *be* the agent rather than be commoditized by one may now matter more to terminal value than Indian GDP growth does.

### Revised rating: HOLD (accumulate on weakness below ~$45)

Downgrading from the original Strong Buy. Reasoning:

- The core demand thesis is intact -- ~20% cc bookings growth through IndiGo disruption, West Asia conflict, $110 Brent, and a 10% rupee move is genuine resilience, not a broken business.
- But the original Buy rested on multiple *expansion* toward a BKNG-like re-rating. That case cannot be underwritten while three unresolved binary items sit open: the NCLAT appeal, whether the alleged second CCI investigation surfaces, and the MMT India IPO price.
- The stock has already run 16%+ off the late-July lows into that uncertainty. Entering here pays up ahead of the catalysts rather than being paid to wait through them.

**What would move this back to Buy:** NCLAT appeal resolved without material additional liability *and* MMT India IPO priced at a premium to the implied parent valuation *and* two consecutive quarters of IFRS (not adjusted) net income growth.

**What would move this to Sell:** confirmation of the second CCI investigation with adverse findings; evidence of actual hotel-side share loss in reported adjusted margins; or FY27 cc bookings growth decelerating below ~12%.

**Question this thesis still hasn't answered, unchanged since Oct 2024:** the comparison to Booking's $130bn market cap. Eighteen months later, the market has voted "not at that multiple." Whether that's a verdict on the multiple or on the asset is the whole investment.

[^1]: MMT has a 56% market share in India. Booking.com has a 69% market share in the hotel industry.
[^2]: This is an assumption.
